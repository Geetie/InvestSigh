11. About This Series
1. Why Packaging Is the Bottleneck
Part 1 covered how CoWoS is built, Part 2 the S/R/L variants, and Part 3 its physical relationship with HBM.
This installment is about supply. However good the technology is, it means nothing if you can't build it.
In 2025, advanced packaging overtook conventional packaging for the first time, at 51.3% market share. And the critical bottleneck in this market is CoWoS.
To build one AI accelerator, you need:
Logic die (GPU/TPU) — fabbed on TSMC's leading-edge nodes
HBM stacks — made by SK hynix / Samsung / Micron
CoWoS packaging — integrating the two into a single chip
No matter how many logic dies and HBM stacks you have, if CoWoS packaging capacity falls short, no finished product ships. From 2024 to today, this has been the real bottleneck in AI silicon.
2. TSMC — The Runaway Leader's Capacity Push
TSMC controls ~80% of the CoWoS market — the result of over a decade of process maturity and yield learning, plus ecosystem lock-in: NVIDIA, AMD, and Broadcom are already TSMC foundry customers.

Fab construction that used to take 3–5 years has been compressed to 1.5–2 years. The investment scale is unprecedented too — of 2026's total CapEx of $52–56B, 20%, roughly $10–11B, goes to packaging and test.
By the numbers: TSMC's advanced packaging revenue in 2025 was about 8–10% of total revenue ($122.4B), i.e. $10–12B. It looks small as a share of revenue — but without it, no NVIDIA GPU makes it into the world.
3. Who Gets CoWoS — Allocation by Customer
CoWoS capacity is not first come, first served. It is allocated by strategic importance and volume-commitment contracts.
The headline: NVIDIA takes 60% of total capacity — including 70% of CoWoS-L, because every Blackwell- and Rubin-generation GPU is built on CoWoS-L.
Broadcom handles the packaging for hyperscaler custom silicon — Google TPU (~90,000 wafers), Meta MTIA (~50,000), and OpenAI custom chips (~10,000).
AMD secured ~105,000 wafers a year for the MI355X and MI400 series, but is routing part of that (~25,000) to sourcing outside TSMC. In other words, the CoWoS shortage is a direct constraint on AMD's AI GPU shipments.
What it means: securing CoWoS capacity is the ticket into the AI chip market. NVIDIA locking up volume early is one of the key reasons it keeps its grip on the GPU market. Technology alone doesn't win — you win by locking up production capacity first.
4. Intel EMIB — The Strongest Alternative
With CoWoS capacity scarce, Intel EMIB is surging as the alternative. This has been the single most noteworthy shift since H2 2025.
What EMIB Is
EMIB (Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge) is Intel's 2.5D packaging technology. Where CoWoS covers the full package area with a silicon interposer, EMIB embeds small silicon bridges only between chips.

Key point: Intel EMIB can also package logic dies fabbed at TSMC. That means a customer can build the chip at TSMC and hand only the packaging to Intel — a brand-new revenue path for Intel Foundry Services.
5. Samsung — Vertical Integration, the Only Card Left

Investment Scale
Samsung's 2026 semiconductor investment is 110 trillion KRW ($73.2B) — up 128% from 47.5 trillion KRW in 2025, the largest semiconductor investment ever by a single company.
US advanced packaging facility: $7B planned (believed to be tied to Tesla-bound packaging)
Yokohama R&D center, Japan: $170M, opening March 2027. Dedicated to packaging research
That said, most of the 110 trillion KRW is believed to go to memory (HBM). Foundry-specific investment actually shrank — $3.5B as of 2025.
Samsung's Dilemma
On paper, Samsung's vertical integration is perfect. In reality, the AI chip big three (NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom) are all TSMC foundry customers, and packaging at TSMC is the natural default.
Even TSMC's overflow doesn't go to Samsung — it goes to OSAT partners like Amkor and ASE. Growing your subcontractors beats feeding volume to a competitor.
To break this structure, Samsung has two plays: catch customers who couldn't get TSMC capacity, or differentiate with a complete package that integrates its own HBM4 into I-Cube.

ASE/SPIL — Enter CoWoP
ASE Group (including SPIL) is developing CoWoP (Chip-on-Wafer-on-PCB) together with NVIDIA — a next-generation format that swaps the CoWoS substrate for PCB, and could eventually complement or replace CoWoS.
Market structure: in advanced packaging as of 2025, OSATs (ASE, Amkor, JCET) hold 59% and foundries/IDMs (TSMC, Samsung, Intel) 39%. The OSAT role keeps growing.
7. Why HBM Is Accelerating the Supply War
Part 3 showed that HBM physically cannot function without CoWoS. Now let's look at the mechanism by which that physical necessity turns into a supply bottleneck.
Exploding HBM Demand = Exploding CoWoS Demand
HBM is projected to top 30% of total DRAM revenue in 2026 (just 8% by shipment volume)
At the same density, HBM consumes ~3x the wafer capacity of DDR5
HBM prices are expected to rise another 15–20% in 2026
SK hynix and Micron HBM capacity is fully sold out through 2026
The point is this: every single HBM stack has to sit on CoWoS. The more HBM gets produced, the more CoWoS demand grows in direct proportion.
Why HBM4 Deepens the Bottleneck
As covered in Part 3, HBM4 demands more interposer area. The CoWoS-S → CoWoS-L transition becomes mandatory, and a larger interposer means fewer chips per wafer — fewer finished products out of the same capacity.
Even if you quadruple capacity, when each chip needs twice the area, effective output only doubles.
8. The Next Battleground — CoPoS and Panel-Level Packaging
Today's CoWoS is packaged on 300mm round wafers. Because the wafer is round, a lot of edge area goes to waste — about 57% area utilization.
TSMC's answer is CoPoS (Chip on Panel on Substrate).

The current winner is TSMC. But this is less a technology lead than ten years of accumulated volume-production experience plus customer ecosystem lock-in. If Intel scales EMIB production through 2027, advanced packaging is likely to consolidate into a TSMC-vs-Intel duopoly.
Samsung is playing its one card — vertical integration — to survive on its own, but prying customers loose from the TSMC ecosystem is the core challenge.
And the fuel for this war is HBM. As long as the chain of AI demand → HBM demand → packaging demand holds unbroken, the supply war goes on.

10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
What is TSMC's current CoWoS capacity?
As of late 2025, 75,000–80,000 wafers per month, expanding to 120,000–130,000 per month by the end of 2026 — roughly a 4x increase over 2024. New packaging fabs are ramping across Taiwan: AP5 (Taichung), AP6 (Zhunan), AP7 (Chiayi), and AP8 (Tainan).
How much CoWoS capacity does NVIDIA take?
In 2026, NVIDIA is estimated to have secured about 60% of total CoWoS capacity (roughly 595,000 wafers per year). It has locked up 70% of CoWoS-L capacity in particular, which it uses for Blackwell- and Rubin-generation GPU production.
Can Intel EMIB replace CoWoS?
Intel EMIB is emerging as the strongest alternative to CoWoS. Google (TPU v9), Amazon, Apple, Qualcomm, and MediaTek are all evaluating EMIB adoption. That said, in production scale and yield, EMIB likely needs another 2–3 years before it can fully replace TSMC CoWoS.
How does Samsung I-Cube compare to CoWoS?
Samsung I-Cube is the only solution that can deliver foundry + memory (HBM) + packaging as a one-stop offering. Its biggest strength is independent supply with no dependence on TSMC. However, it trails TSMC CoWoS in production scale and yield, and the major AI chip customers (NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom) are locked into the TSMC ecosystem, so winning customers remains the challenge.
How long will the CoWoS shortage last?
The structural shortage is expected to persist through 2027. TSMC is quadrupling capacity, but AI accelerator demand is growing even faster. The HBM4 transition also enlarges packaging area, reducing the number of chips per wafer — a factor that deepens the bottleneck further.
How are HBM demand and CoWoS supply related?
Every HBM stack must sit on advanced packaging like CoWoS. As HBM grew past 30% of DRAM revenue, packaging demand exploded with it. Because HBM consumes about 3x the wafer capacity of DDR5 at the same density, rising HBM demand means a deepening CoWoS bottleneck.
What is TSMC's next-generation packaging technology?
CoPoS (Chip on Panel on Substrate). It replaces the 300mm round wafer with a 310×310mm square glass panel, raising area utilization from 57% to 87%. The pilot line completes in June 2026, with volume production targeted for 2028–2029. It is aimed at the generations after NVIDIA Rubin.
